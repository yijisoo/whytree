# Product Seed Questions Reference

These seed a Why Tree for **product feature definition** — for a builder shaping a product's direction and eliciting its highest-priority features. The technique is identical to the life domain (Why Up to surface purpose, How Down to surface means); only the entry points differ.

Use 1–2 seeds per session — not all of them. Which seeds fit depends on the builder's **stage** (see `framing.md`): idea-stage (nothing built yet) vs has-built (a shipped or in-progress product). When introducing a seed, name its mechanism in one sentence — it signals you know why you're asking and earns more honest answers.

## One tree, two seed sources

A product seed comes from one of two sources, and the tree holds both on the same structure:

- **Builder pull** — a thing the builder keeps wanting to make ("a thing I keep wanting to build").
- **Customer need** — an observed struggle in the people they'd serve ("a struggle I keep seeing").

A product's high purpose usually serves the customer's purpose too, so a builder-desire branch and a customer-need branch tend to **converge** as they climb. That convergence is the strong signal — the thing is both genuinely *wanted* (by the builder) and genuinely *needed* (by a customer). A branch that climbs only to builder-why (status, fear, sunk cost) and never touches a customer why is the **risk branch** — the finding, not a failure. (See `framing.md` for how to run the convergence test as a probe.)

**Evidence provenance (ask on every customer-source seed and every customer `why`):** *"Is this from a specific person or observation, or is it your assumption about them?"* A customer need recalled from a real person or watched moment is stronger evidence than one the builder rationalized. Convergence between a builder-desire branch and a builder-*imagined* customer branch can manufacture a confident-feeling false positive — say the distinction aloud and treat assumption-grounded convergence as a weaker signal than observation-grounded.

## The seeds (tagged by stage)

**0a. The Prior-Attempts Question** *(any stage — the strongest seed source)* — "What have you started building before — shipped or abandoned?"
   - Targets **durable pull** — what someone has actually attempted reveals their real why better than asking "what do you want to build?" in the abstract.
   - Seed each past attempt as its own node, run Why Up on each, and watch where they converge: the through-line across every attempt is the builder's real why.
   - Abandoned attempts are data too — the obstacle-seed move carries over from the life domain.
   - Introduce as: *"Before we talk about what's next — what have you actually tried to build before? Finished or not."*

**0b. The Unfinished-Pull Question** *(any stage)* — "What's the project you never finished but keep returning to?"
   - Targets **persistent intrinsic pull** — the idea that won't let go usually sits closest to a real purpose.
   - Introduce as: *"Is there something you keep coming back to — started, dropped, but it keeps tugging at you?"*

**RE. The Reverse-Engineer Question** *(has-built — see `framing.md` Reverse-engineer mode)* — "Walk me through what you built. Why does this part exist?"
   - Targets **the implicit purpose behind shipped features** — a built product is already a set of `how` nodes (its features are concrete means). Run Why Up from the existing features to climb to the purpose the builder never spelled out, then How Down from that purpose to surface features they haven't considered.
   - The generative payoff is surfacing purposes that were never conscious. The counseling stance matters: framing non-convergence as *information, not a verdict* (see `framing.md`).
   - Introduce as: *"Walk me through the main thing you've built. Pick one piece — why does that piece exist?"*

**1. The Builder-Desire Question** *(idea-stage)* — "What do you keep wanting to build, even when no one asked?"
   - Targets **builder pull** — the unprompted urge to make a specific thing.
   - Introduce as: *"Not what the market wants — what *you* keep wanting to build, request or no request."*

**3. The Admiration Question** *(idea-stage)* — "What product do you admire enough to wish you'd built it?"
   - Targets **values via admiration** — what you envy reveals what you value building toward.
   - Follow-up: *"What about it do you wish were yours — the thing it does, or the way it does it?"*
   - Introduce as: *"What's a product you admire enough that you wish you'd been the one to build it?"*

**4. The Workaround Question** *(any stage — customer source)* — "What clumsy workaround do you see people forced into?"
   - Targets **customer need (jobs-to-be-done)** — a workaround is a job someone is hiring a bad tool to do.
   - Apply the evidence-provenance probe: a workaround you've *watched* someone do is stronger than one you assume happens.
   - Introduce as: *"Where do you see people doing something clumsy — duct-taping a process — because nothing good exists?"*

**5. The Complaint Question** *(any stage — customer source)* — "What complaint do you keep hearing from the people you'd serve?"
   - Targets **live customer why** — a recurring complaint is a need stated out loud.
   - Apply the evidence-provenance probe: a complaint from a *named* person beats a generalized "people say."
   - Introduce as: *"From the people you'd serve — what's the complaint you keep hearing?"*

**7. The Attachment Question** *(has-built — tension / risk branch)* — "What feature are you attached to but can't say why a user would care?"
   - Targets **the risk branch** — a feature the builder loves but can't ground in a customer why is builder-desire that never converges. (This question came directly out of building Why Tree itself.)
   - Surfacing it has an emotional flip side: the builder may discover a piece of their own product doesn't serve a purpose they value. Frame it as insight, not indictment.
   - Introduce as: *"Is there a feature you're attached to but, if you're honest, you can't quite say why a user would care?"*

**Gaps are intentional.** The numbering (0a, 0b, RE, 1, 3, 4, 5, 7) mirrors the life-pack seed indices so a product seed can be traced to the life seed it adapts; 2 and 6 are life seeds with no useful product analog and are deliberately left empty.

**High-volume openers.** When a builder names more than 3–4 candidate seeds (attempts, complaints, desires) at once, do not seed all of them. Seed the 2–3 with the most charge — where the voice changes, where detail arrives unprompted — and name the rest as threads to hold for later.

These are drafts. Final product seed questions need stress-test evidence, the way the life pack was validated; refine them between sessions.
